The "kids-bicycle-with-training-wheels" modesty aside, an 8B and 70B parameter model trained across 1,000+ languages with entirely open weights, data, and alignment methods is a formidable piece of engineering. The Swiss AI Initiative is building something genuinely vital here: true open-source sovereignty.

However, your current website architecture is hiding its light under an academic bushel. The current navigation is structured like a traditional software repository rather than a welcoming ecosystem. If the goal is to make Apertus inclusive, accessible, and community-driven, the digital front door needs a fundamental remodel to route users by *intent* rather than by *artifact*.

Here is a critical, professional review of the Apertus website structure and a proposed navigation framework for your communications team.

## 1. Current State Audit

**Current Navigation:** `Get Started` | `Documentation` | `Research` | `About` | `Contact`
**Current Hero CTAs:** `Get Started` | `Showcase`

**The Critical Gaps:**

* **The "Cold Start" Problem:** "Get Started" is highly ambiguous. To a developer, it means API keys and GitHub repos. To an enthusiast, it means a chat interface. The current structure forces users to guess what lives behind that button.
* **Hidden Ecosystem:** Your goal is to get people trying community-built apps and inference endpoints. Currently, "Showcase" is relegated to a secondary hero button and absent from the main navigation. It lacks persistence as users navigate the site.
* **Missing Contribution Pathways:** You want to encourage open-source contributions, but there is no dedicated "Community" or "Contribute" hub. Open-source thrives on visibility; contributors shouldn't have to dig through documentation to find out how to help.
* **Academic Overhang:** Terms like "Sovereign AI" and "Alignment Principles" are powerful but can feel exclusionary to newcomers. The site needs progressive disclosure—start with accessible concepts and let experts drill down into the rigorous science.

---

## 2. Proposed Navigation Restructure

To build an inclusive ecosystem, restructure the navigation around user personas: the **Curious** (want to try it), the **Builders** (want to use it), the **Academics** (want to study it), and the **Contributors** (want to build it).

| Current Structure | Proposed Structure | Core Audience Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get Started | **Try Apertus** | Enthusiasts, General Public, Product Managers |
| Documentation | **Build & Deploy** | Developers, Engineers, Enterprises |
| Research | **Science & Open Data** | Researchers, Policy Makers, AI Ethicists |
| *Missing* | **Community** | Open Source Contributors, Advocates |
| About / Contact | **Initiative** | Press, Partners, Institutional Stakeholders |

### Detailed Dropdown Architecture

**1. Try Apertus** *(The low-friction entry point)*

* **Model Playground:** A direct link to a hosted inference endpoint to chat with the 8B/70B models immediately.
* **Community Showcase:** A gallery of apps built on Apertus.
* **Inference Providers:** Where to find Apertus hosted in the wild (e.g., the "Inference Ecosystem" mentioned in your September blog post).

**2. Build & Deploy** *(The builder's hub)*

* **Quick Start Guide:** 5-minute setup to run locally or via API.
* **Documentation:** Technical specs, API references, and architecture.
* **Download Weights:** Direct links to Hugging Face or your preferred repository.

**3. Science & Open Data** *(The transparency differentiator)*

* **Publications & Papers:** Centralizing your ACL 2026 paper and future technical reports.
* **Training Data & Methods:** The crown jewel of your open-source claim. Detail the 1,000+ language corpus and PII removal techniques.
* **Alignment & Ethics:** How Apertus handles opt-outs and responsible scaling.

**4. Community** *(The growth engine)*

* **How to Contribute:** Clear, step-by-step guides on contributing code, data, or evaluations.
* **Discussion Forums:** Discord, GitHub Discussions, or mailing lists.
* **Events & Hackathons:** Highlight Swiss AI Initiative events or community meetups.

**5. Initiative** *(The foundation)*

* **About Apertus:** The collaborative vision between EPFL, ETH Zurich, and CSCS.
* **News & Updates:** Elevate your blog posts (like the v1.5 release) from the footer to a proper newsroom.
* **Partners & Contact:** Swisscom and future strategic partners, plus press inquiries.

## 3. Messaging and Inclusivity Recommendations

Restructuring the navigation solves the routing problem, but the content itself must follow suit to hit your accessibility goals.

* **Demystify the Hero Section:** Your current hero text reads: *"Fully Open Foundation Model for Sovereign AI."* This is excellent for institutional partners but opaque to community developers. Consider a slight pivot that emphasizes utility: *"The open foundation model for everyone. Built for performance, trained on 1,000+ languages, and completely transparent from data to deployment."*
* **Implement "Progressive Disclosure":** On pages like "Science & Open Data," lead with a plain-English summary of *why* this matters (e.g., "We believe you should know exactly what your AI learned and how it learned it"), followed by the dense technical links (reproducibility guides, alignment code).
* **Elevate the Global Story:** Being multilingual from day one (1,000+ languages) is a massive inclusivity win. Feature this prominently. Highlight showcase apps that use non-English languages to prove the model's global utility visually.
* **Clarify the "Fully Open" Definition:** "Apertus is to AI as Open is to Source" is a fantastic tagline. Lean into it. Create a visual checklist on the homepage comparing Apertus to "open weights" models, explicitly showing that you open the *data, methods, and alignment* as well.

The foundation you have built is world-class. By pivoting the website from a project repository into an audience-centric ecosystem, you will drastically lower the barrier to entry, increase community adoption, and properly showcase the caliber of research happening in the Alps.
